# Where agent-to-agent commerce already has volume

Paid agent-to-agent volume exists today, but it is small and concentrated in metered per-call endpoints rather than in job marketplaces. The only venue that publishes an on-chain money figure is Olas: $110,565.51 of all-time marketplace turnover against 14,925,097 agent-to-agent transactions, which is roughly $0.007 per transaction. Coinbase's x402 discovery index lists 58,681 payable resources at a sampled median price of $0.01 per call, but publishes no call counts, so supply is measurable and demand is not. Job-style venues publish agent counts, not volume: Virtuals ACP claims over 2,000 agents with no job count, Fetch.ai Agentverse could not be read at all from here (HTTP 403), and NEAR Agent Market's top 50 agents sum to 353 lifetime deliveries. Two things that look like marketplaces are not payment venues at all: Glama is a 114,033-entry catalogue with no payment rail, and Smithery shut its paid MCP proxy on 31 October 2026. The venues with real volume share five traits — accountless payment, cent-level pricing, synchronous settlement for small work, machine-readable discovery, and a published counter someone else can check — and Agent Market currently has only part of the fourth and none of the fifth.

## What the venues with real volume have in common

**1. The buyer pays without an account.** x402 is an HTTP 402 challenge and a signed payment: no signup, no OAuth, no card. Olas buyers are wallets. Every venue in the table that requires a human account to hire has no published demand number. This is the strongest pattern in the data, and it is also the one that is easiest to explain: an autonomous agent cannot complete an OAuth flow that needs a person, so a venue that requires one has already excluded the buyer that scales.

**2. Cent-level price granularity.** The x402 median is $0.01 per call and the 25th percentile is $0.003. Olas' implied $0.007 per agent-to-agent transaction lands in the same band. At this layer commerce is metered, not contracted: a buyer decides per call, not per statement of work.

**3. Synchronous settlement for small work, escrow only for large work.** x402 and Olas settle a single request. Job boards hold money in escrow and wait for acceptance, which is the right shape only when the work outlives one HTTP call. The venues that try to use escrow for cent-sized work carry a settlement cost larger than the transaction.

**4. Machine-readable discovery.** The x402 index and Glama's catalogue are things an agent can enumerate programmatically. Virtuals ACP and Agentverse need a client or a UI, and their volume is the least visible. Discovery that a machine can read is a precondition for demand that a machine can generate.

**5. A published counter someone else can check.** Olas publishes on-chain totals. x402 publishes the resource count but not the calls. Where nothing is published, no one can claim volume, and a buyer has no way to compare venues.

## Which of those traits Agent Market lacks, mechanically

**Signup friction: it lacks accountless buying.** Hiring over MCP requires OAuth, which means a human account mints the token. A2A+x402 is the exception, but it is not the default path. This is the largest gap, because it is the exact trait that separates the venues with published volume from the ones without.

**Pricing granularity: it lacks a price in the machine-readable catalogue.** `/v1/agents` returns `delivered_jobs`, `success_rate`, `sla_seconds` and `payable_with`, but no price or quote field. A buyer agent cannot rank listings by cost without opening each page, so it cannot make the per-call decision that cent-level commerce requires. The x402 index does the opposite: price and `payTo` are in the JSON, but there is no reputation or delivery history.

**Call semantics and latency: the synchronous path exists but is opt-in, and the default path is a multi-day queue.** An A2A+x402 call can block on the HTTP connection for "up to about a minute" waiting for the agent's result, so a fast path does exist (https://market.near.ai/skill/flows/a2a-x402.md, opened 2026-11-02). But it requires the builder to run a webhook backend and a live per-call pricing plan, and underneath it the call is still opened as a hire with the payment held as escrow. The path a normal buyer uses — post a job, award a bid, receive a deliverable, accept — has no synchronous option: acceptance is manual or waits out a three-day window, up to seven (https://docs.market.near.ai/concepts/payments-and-disputes, opened 2026-11-02). The measured numbers are not cent-commerce latency: across the top 50 agents the self-reported response-time median has a median of **46 s** and a p90 of **31,107 s (~8.6 h)**, and the median SLA is **3,600 s** with a p90 of **86,400 s** (computed from https://market.near.ai/v1/agents?limit=50, 2026-11-02). A $0.01 lookup cannot wait an hour; a $15 job cannot settle in a minute.

**Settlement cost: the rail is priced for larger jobs.** A 5% fee on a $0.05 job is $0.0025, while a measured Base USDC transfer on 2026-11-02 cost $0.0181 to $0.0355 in gas (txs `0x0e4f4ddfc25ec92107dbbcda4a1d14e350dae5fc9b6b87ff3fe382644ec36291` and `0x01f3815135e25aa00fc7a25385d968c0bafbb44ec4175e4578caf0f36f2fe9a1`; receipts via https://base-rpc.publicnode.com). The escrow-plus-onchain model therefore has a floor well above the price band where the volume actually is. x402 avoids this by settling per call without a job record; Olas keeps its implied ticket at $0.007 by metering on-chain.

**What it already has.** Machine-readable discovery exists in part (`/v1/agents`, MCP, A2A cards), and a published counter does not exist. The catalogue is readable; the price and the demand counter are not.

## Gaps

- No venue publishes paid call counts. x402 exposes 58,681 endpoints and zero demand data; Virtuals ACP exposes 2,000+ agents and zero jobs; Agentverse is behind a 403. Only Olas has a money figure, and it is all-time, not per day.
- Price is missing from NEAR's machine-readable catalogue, and reputation is missing from x402's.
- Glama and Smithery are discovery surfaces, not payment venues; treating them as commerce overstates the market.
- Data quality in the x402 index is poor: the sampled maximum price is 10,000,000,000 USDC and several entries carry no description, so the median is robust and the tail is not.
- Virtuals ACP's fee and settlement asset, Agentverse's volume, and any per-day figure for x402 or NEAR could not be grounded, so they are not estimated here.

## What Agent Market should do next

1. **Make the A2A+x402 call path the default for small work.** It already exists; enable it on every listing, price it at cents rather than the documented $12.00 example, and keep the one-minute blocking contract, because every venue with published volume lets the buyer pay without a human account and the venues that require OAuth publish no demand. Evidence: x402's 58,681 resources and Olas' wallet-based buying in the table above, plus the one-minute blocking contract in https://market.near.ai/skill/flows/a2a-x402.md.
2. **Put a price and a quote field in `/v1/agents`**, so a buyer agent can rank listings by cost without opening each page. Evidence: the catalogue returns `delivered_jobs` and `sla_seconds` but no price; x402's index carries price and `payTo` in JSON.
3. **Set a minimum escrow job price at or above the payout cost, and route everything below it to the synchronous path**, because a 5% fee on $0.05 is $0.0025 against a measured $0.0181-$0.0355 Base transfer. Evidence: the fee in the marketplace docs and the measured transfers.
4. **Publish a demand counter that a third party can check** — paid calls per day and settled jobs per day, with the method — because the only venue with a verifiable money figure is also the only one whose volume anyone can cite. Evidence: Olas' on-chain totals versus x402's resource-only count.
5. **Instrument which rail each job actually uses** (escrow, A2A+x402, MCP) before adding features, because the table shows the two rails have different price floors and the current catalogue does not say which one a listing supports beyond `payable_with`. Evidence: `payable_with` is USDC or USD+USDC, with no rail or latency field.
